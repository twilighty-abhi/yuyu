Some events, usually hackathons, ask each team or solo participant to submit a project for judging. You write a short description, add links to your code, demo and video, and submit it before the deadline. You can keep editing until submissions close.

## Who can submit

Each **entrant** has one project:

- on a team event, the **team** shares one project
- if you take part on your own, the project is **yours**

Only confirmed registrations can submit. If you're still waitlisted or waiting for approval, the project section says it opens once your place is confirmed.

## Open your project

Where you edit the project depends on how you registered.

| You are | Open the project from |
| --- | --- |
| A team leader | Your team page (the link in your team email). |
| A team member, signed in | **Account settings** → **Registrations** → **Team project**, or **Open team project** on your ticket. |
| On your own, signed in | **Account settings** → **Registrations** → **Project**, or **Open your project** on your ticket. |
| On your own, registered as a guest | Your ticket → **Email me my submission link**. |

A team member who isn't signed in sees "Your team leader submits the project from the team page." Any member can still edit the project by signing in to the account they registered with.

### The emailed submission link

If you registered on your own without an account, choose **Email me my submission link** on your ticket. We send a private link to the email address you registered with. Anyone with that link can edit your project, so keep it to yourself. Asking again sends a new link and the old one stops working; you can ask once a minute.

## Write and submit your project

<Steps>

1. Enter a **Project title** (up to 120 characters).
2. Add a **Summary** of what you built and how it works: plain text, up to 4,000 characters. It's optional, but judges rely on it.
3. Optionally add links to your **Code repository**, **Demo** and **Video**. Each must be a full `https://` link.
4. Choose **Save draft** to keep working on it later, or **Submit project** when it's ready.

</Steps>

When you submit, you see "Project submitted" and we email you a confirmation. The project's status chip changes from **Draft** to **Submitted**.

Judges see your title, summary, links and your team name (or, on your own, your first name and last initial). They never see your email address.

## Edit or withdraw

While submissions are open you can keep improving a submitted project: make your changes and choose **Save changes**. It stays submitted.

To take it out of judging, choose **Withdraw…** and confirm with **Withdraw**. A withdrawn project isn't judged. You can bring it back with **Submit again** while submissions are still open.

## The submission window

The chip at the top of the project page shows where things stand:

| Chip | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| "Submissions open …" | Submissions haven't started yet. You can't start the project until then. |
| "Submissions close …" | Submissions are open. Save and submit before the time shown. |
| "Submissions are closed." | You can't submit or change the project any more. A submitted project stays in judging. |
| "The organisers haven't set the submission dates yet." | Check back later. |

Dates are shown in the event's time zone. The deadline is checked when you save, so submit in good time rather than at the last second.

<Callout kind="warning">

A draft isn't judged. If submissions close while your project is still a draft or withdrawn, it can't be submitted.

</Callout>

## After submissions close

Depending on the organisers' choices, the event may then publish a **project gallery** of submitted projects and, after judging, a **results** page with the awards and winning projects. Scores and judges' comments are never shown publicly. If results are published, everyone with a confirmed place gets an email.

## Good to know

- If the organisers disqualify a project, its page says "This project isn't part of judging." Contact the organisers if you have questions.
- If you registered on your own with a submitted project and then join a team, withdraw your solo project first. A solo draft is discarded when you join a team.
- Disbanding a team, or cancelling a solo registration, removes its project too.
- Your projects are included when you [download your data](/help/account/your-data#download-your-data).
