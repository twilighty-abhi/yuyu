Yuyu can run the competitive side of a hackathon, game jam or pitch night: teams and solo entrants submit a project, judges score each one against your rubric, and you announce the winners on a public results page. This article shows how the pieces fit together. The other articles in this section cover each step in detail.

<Callout kind="permission">

Setting up and running judging (submissions, rubric, judges, awards and results) is for owners and admins of the organisation only. No co-organiser permission reaches it, not even **Registrations**, because judging decides the outcome of the competition.

Judges can be owners and admins, or co-organisers you invite with the **Judge** permission. See [Judges, rubric and scoring](/help/hackathons/judging).

</Callout>

## What you need

- A one-off event. Submissions aren't available on the dates of a recurring series.
- Either registration mode. On a team event, each team enters one project. On an individual event, each person enters their own. See [Setting up team registration](/help/registration/team-registration).
- A live event. Entrants can only see the submission form once the event is published.

## Turning on submissions

<Steps>

1. Open the event and choose the **Settings** tab.
2. In the **Project submissions** card, turn on **Projects and judging**.
3. Set when submissions **Opens** and **Closes**. Times are in the event's time zone.
4. Choose **Save settings**.

</Steps>

A new **Judging** tab appears on the event dashboard, and the submission settings move there. From now on, everything about the competition lives in that tab.

If the event is still a draft, Yuyu warns you: entrants can't see the form until you publish the event from the **Overview** tab. See [Publishing and visibility](/help/events/publishing).

## The Judging tab

The **Judging** tab is split into sections:

| Section | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| **Setup** | The submission window, which projects judges see, the public gallery, opening and closing judging, and the rubric. |
| **Submissions** | Every project, with filters, search, judge assignments and disqualification. |
| **Judges** | Your invited judges, and the owners and admins who can also judge. |
| **Progress** | Judges' progress, projects that need more reviews, and automatic assignment. |
| **Score** | Where you score projects yourself, if you're judging too. |
| **Results** | The ranked projects, the CSV export and publishing the results page. |
| **Awards** | Your awards and the projects that win them. |

## From start to finish

A typical competition runs like this:

1. **Prepare.** Turn on submissions, set the window, build the rubric, and invite judges. See [Judges, rubric and scoring](/help/hackathons/judging).
2. **Collect projects.** Confirmed teams and solo entrants submit during the window from their ticket or team page. See [Submissions and the gallery](/help/hackathons/submissions).
3. **Share out the work.** If judges should only see some projects, assign them by hand or automatically. See [Assigning judges and tracking progress](/help/hackathons/progress).
4. **Open judging.** Judges score each project against the rubric and submit a review.
5. **Close judging.** The scores are frozen and the ranking is final.
6. **Pick winners and publish.** Add awards, choose winners from the ranked list, and publish the results page. Every confirmed guest gets an email. See [Awards and results](/help/hackathons/results).

## Judging status

Judging moves through three states, shown on the **Judging** card in **Setup**:

- **Not started.** Judges can read submissions but not score them. You can still change the rubric.
- **Open.** Judges score and submit reviews. The rubric is locked.
- **Closed.** Scores are frozen. You can pick winners and publish results.

You can reopen judging after closing it, until the results are published.

<Callout kind="warning">

Once judging has started or results are published, you can't turn **Projects and judging** off again, because that would hide the results, the gallery and the judges' work. To stop taking new projects, move the closing time instead.

</Callout>

## What stays private

Judges see each project's title, summary, links and the team or entrant name. They never see entrants' email addresses, registration answers or ticket links, and they never see a project they're registered on themselves.

The public pages only show project titles, names, links and awards. Scores, ranks and judges' comments are never public.
