With a handful of projects, every judge can review everything. With dozens, you'll want to share the work out so each project gets enough reviews without anyone drowning. This article covers assigning judges, by hand or automatically, and the **Progress** section that shows where judging stands.

<Callout kind="permission">

Owners and admins of the organisation. Judges don't see assignments or progress, only their own list of projects to score.

</Callout>

## Switching to assigned projects

By default, **All judges see all submissions**. To give each judge only some projects, go to **Judging** → **Setup**, choose **Only assigned submissions** under **Which projects judges see**, and choose **Save settings**.

Assigning judges automatically (below) makes this switch for you.

## Assigning judges by hand

<Steps>

1. Open **Judging** → **Submissions**.
2. Choose **⋯** on a submitted project, then **Assign judges**.
3. Tick the judges who should review it, then choose **Done**.

</Steps>

With **Only assigned submissions** on, each submitted project lists its judges, or "none assigned". Judges registered on a project can't be assigned to it.

## Assigning judges automatically

Yuyu can share the submitted projects out so that each one gets the same number of judges and everyone's load stays even.

<Steps>

1. Open **Judging** → **Progress** and choose **Assign judges automatically**.
2. Set **Judges per project**, from 1 to 20. It starts at your target (3 if you haven't set one).
3. Under **Current assignments**, choose **Keep current assignments** to only fill the gaps, or **Start over** to share everything out again.
4. Under **Who judges**, tick the judges to use. Invited judges are ticked already. Owners and admins only judge if you tick them.
5. Choose **Preview**.
6. Check the preview, then choose **Apply assignment**.

</Steps>

The preview changes nothing. It shows each judge's assignments now, the change, and the total after, and lists any projects that can't reach the number because there aren't enough judges without a conflict of interest.

When you apply:

- Judges switch to seeing only their assigned projects.
- **Start over** keeps any assignment where the judge has already saved scores, so nobody loses work in progress.
- If a judge already started scoring a project they weren't assigned, it becomes their assignment.
- The number you chose becomes the event's target for **Progress**.

Running it again with nothing changed gives the same result. If anything changes between your preview and applying, Yuyu asks you to preview again.

Assignments can't change once judging is closed.

## Tracking progress

**Judging** → **Progress** shows how close judging is to done.

<Screenshot name="judging-progress" alt="The Judging progress section with the summary numbers, the target field and the list of projects below target" />

The summary at the top shows:

- **Projects at target**: how many projects have enough submitted reviews.
- **Review coverage**: submitted reviews as a share of what you're aiming for.
- **Outlier scores** and **Stale reviews**: how many need a look (see below).

Set **Target judges per project** to the number of reviews you want for each project, between 1 and 20, and choose **Save target**. Leave it empty for the default of 3.

Use the chips to show **All lists** or just one of them:

- **Below target**: submitted projects with fewer reviews than the target, fewest first. Send judges here first.
- **Judges**: each judge's assigned projects, how many they've started and submitted, and their completion, lowest first.
- **Outliers**: a judge whose score for a project is far from the other judges' average (more than two standard deviations), or, when only two judges reviewed it, scores more than 25 points apart. It's worth a second look, but nothing changes automatically.
- **Stale**: reviews with saved scores that haven't been submitted for over 24 hours. Unsubmitted reviews don't count towards the results, so nudge those judges.

Each list shows 25 rows to a page. Only submitted reviews from people who can still judge, and who aren't registered on the project, count.

## Good to know

- The **Views** menu saves the list you're looking at, such as **Below target**. See [Filters and saved views](/help/guests/saved-views).
- To see each judge's scores and comments for a project, use **Judges' reviews** on the **Submissions** section. See [Submissions and the gallery](/help/hackathons/submissions).
- Before closing judging, check **Below target** and **Stale** are empty, or that you're happy with what's there. See [Judges, rubric and scoring](/help/hackathons/judging#closing-judging).
